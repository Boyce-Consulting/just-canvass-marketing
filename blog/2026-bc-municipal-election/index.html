2026 BC Municipal Election: Local Election Dates & a Candidate's Guide

Updated July 2026 · British Columbia

British Columbia's 2026 general local elections are Saturday, October 17, 2026. Across the province, voters elect mayors, councillors, school trustees, and regional district electoral area directors on the same day. BC does things a little differently from the rest of Canada: each local government runs its own vote, while Elections BC polices the money side province-wide — and the official nomination window is famously short.

Here's the BC candidate's calendar, with the nomination period, eligibility rules, and campaign-finance deadlines, plus how to plan your canvassing around a tight fall timeline.

Confirm before you file. Election day and the campaign-finance rules are province-wide, but nomination handling, voting places, and local requirements are set by your local government. Always confirm with your municipality and Elections BC before you act. Last verified July 2026.

Election day: Saturday, October 17, 2026

British Columbia holds general local elections every four years on the third Saturday of October. In 2026 that's October 17. Unlike Ontario and Manitoba, BC votes on a Saturday, and the offices on the ballot include not just mayors and councillors but regional district electoral area directors and, in many areas, school trustees.

Who can run

To run in a BC local election you must, at the time you file, be:

  • a Canadian citizen;
  • at least 18 years old on general voting day;
  • a resident of British Columbia for at least six months immediately before filing your nomination; and
  • not disqualified under the Local Government Act or any other law from being nominated for, elected to, or holding office.

Depending on the office, additional local residency or property-based rules can apply. Confirm the specifics for the seat you're seeking with your local government.

Nominations: September 1 to 11, 2026

BC's nomination period is short and late in the calendar: it opens September 1, 2026 and closes at 4:00 p.m. on Friday, September 11, 2026. Each local government accepts and processes its own nominations, so filing locations, hours, and any local documentation are set locally.

  • Who runs the vote: each local government administers its own election — voting, counting, reporting results, and accepting nominations.
  • Campaign financing & advertising: administered province-wide by Elections BC under the Local Elections Campaign Financing Act. This covers contribution limits, expense limits, and third-party advertising.
  • Disclosure after the vote: candidates file a campaign-financing disclosure statement with Elections BC by January 15, 2027, with a late option to February 16, 2027 that carries a $500 late-filing fee. Missing the deadline can bar you from running next time, so treat it as seriously as nomination day.

See Elections BC's 2026 General Local Elections page for the campaign-financing rules, and your municipality's website for local nomination details.

Frequently asked questions

When is the 2026 local election in British Columbia?

British Columbia's 2026 general local elections are on Saturday, October 17, 2026. The nomination period runs from September 1 to September 11, 2026, with nominations closing at 4:00 p.m. on Friday, September 11.

Who can run in a BC local election?

You must be a Canadian citizen, at least 18 years old on general voting day, a resident of British Columbia for at least six months before filing your nomination, and not disqualified under the Local Government Act or any other law. Local residency or property rules may also apply depending on the office.

Who runs local elections in British Columbia?

Each local government runs its own election — voting, counting, reporting results, and accepting nominations. Elections BC administers the campaign-financing and election-advertising rules province-wide under the Local Elections Campaign Financing Act.

When are campaign finance disclosure statements due in BC?

For the 2026 general local elections, candidates must file their disclosure statement with Elections BC by January 15, 2027. A late-filing option is available until February 16, 2027 with a $500 late-filing fee.

When should I start canvassing for a 2026 BC campaign?

Start well before the September nomination period. Because that window is short, successful candidates build their team, message, and turf plan through spring and summer, then run an intense door-knocking, phone, text and GOTV push through the fall to election day.
